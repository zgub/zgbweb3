# Energy ladder — reference list

The shared list behind the Energy scales topic and every energy comparison on
the site (D-026). One value per rung, in joules, with its assumptions and
where the number comes from. Comparisons only use rungs from this list, so
they always agree with each other. Peter adds rungs over time.

**Range.** The main ladder runs from **1 eV to 1 foe (10⁴⁴ J)**, about
63 orders of magnitude: from a single chemical bond to a supernova, or to
everything the Sun will shine in its lifetime. Beyond that is an "edge of the
map" annex. Its numbers are real but rougher, and it ends with a note on why
there is no single number for the Big Bang.

**Status column.** *exact* = by definition; *derived* = computed from
constants or the stated assumptions; *sourced* = taken from the source named;
*typical* = a representative value, real items vary; *estimate* = the
literature gives a range.

**Sources.** Where a link is given it was checked on 2026-10-21. Sources
marked "link to check" are standard references named from knowledge; their
links still need checking before the page goes live.

| 70.1 | Rest energy of all ordinary matter in the observable universe | ~10⁷⁰ | ≈ 1.5 × 10⁵³ kg; order of magnitude only | estimate |

**Why the ladder stops there.** "The energy of the Big Bang" sounds like the
next rung, but there is no such number. In general relativity, energy is
conserved locally but there is no agreed way to add up a total energy for an
expanding universe. So the honest top of the ladder is "everything we can see,
as mass-energy", not a figure for the beginning.

## Below the bottom rung (optional)

If a "basement" is wanted later: the thermal energy of one molecule at room
temperature, kT ≈ 0.025 eV, is a natural floor, and it links straight to
thermodynamics.

## Pairs that surprise (for the topic text)

Computed from the rungs above:
- A full phone battery ≈ one teaspoon of sugar (≈ 70 kJ each).
- One cosmic-ray nucleus ≈ a thrown baseball (≈ 51 J).
- A flying mosquito ≈ 1 TeV; one LHC proton ≈ 5 flying mosquitoes.
- The ISS in orbit ≈ 1.6 full 747 tanks.
- A fully fuelled Starship stack ≈ 6.9 full 747 tanks ≈ 12 kt TNT.
- A Starship stack in household-days of electricity: about 2.1 million days if
  a household uses 2500 kWh a year. The household figure still needs a source (Q-016).
- Sunlight reaching the Earth each second ≈ the largest bomb ever tested.
- Everything the Sun will shine in its life ≈ one supernova blast.

## Method notes
- Kinetic energy: E = ½ m v². Battery: E = capacity × nominal voltage.
  Fuel: mass × lower heating value. Photon: E = hc/λ. Rest energy: E = mc².
- Calculations were run in code on 2026-10-21; rounding to 2–3 significant
  figures is deliberate.
- When a rung is added, record its value, assumptions, status and source here
  first, then add it to the app.
